\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We apply MH-DIF to the METABENCH response matrix~\citep{kipnis2024metabench} (5,227 models $\times$ 12,508 MMLU items~\citep{hendrycks2021mmlu}), with temporal cohorts defined by release date (2023 vs.\ 2024). Cross-benchmark replication on GSM8K appears in \Cref{sec:gsm8k}.

\subsection{Validation: DIF Detection Power}
\label{sec:validation}

A power analysis using empirical MMLU IRT parameters confirms that at $N \geq 80$ per cohort, ETS~C achieves TPR of 0.68--0.80, FPR of 0.036--0.042, and AUC of 0.93--0.94 for items with $|\Delta_{\mathrm{MH}}| \geq 1.4$. Our temporal cohorts ($N_{\mathrm{ref}} = 1{,}080$, $N_{\mathrm{focal}} = 807$) far exceed this threshold.

\subsection{Temporal Comparability: 31\% Item Instability}
\label{sec:temporal}

Comparing 2023 ($N = 1{,}080$) against 2024 ($N = 807$) models, 31.3\% of items receive ETS~C classification ($|\Delta_{\mathrm{MH}}| \geq 1.5$). Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction at $q = 0.05$ removes only 3 items (31.32\% $\to$ 31.30\%), because the dual ETS threshold on effect size and significance already provides implicit multiple-testing protection.

Three controls confirm this reflects genuine temporal change. A random split of the 2023 cohort yields only 0.18\% ETS~C items (sampling noise), and an ability-quintile comparison within the same temporal cohort yields 2.9\% (ability differences alone). A within-family comparison (Llama-2 vs.\ Llama-3) produces 61.3\% ETS~C items, elevated by large ability gaps that confound the DIF signal~\citep{holland1988mh}. The contrast between 2.9\% (ability-controlled) and 61.3\% (ability-confounded) brackets the temporal rate of 31.3\%.

Across all 57 MMLU subjects, C\% ranges from 20.7\% (computer security) to 48.4\% (business ethics) with unweighted mean $\approx 30\%$. Within-subject matching on 5 representative subjects yields a mean of 38.1\% (range: 31--45\%), reported in \Cref{tab:temporal_landscape} as a robustness check for the matching protocol. Despite an ecological correlation of $\rho = 0.307$ ($p = 0.02$) between subject-level C\% and accuracy improvement, item-level logistic regression reveals that domain accuracy improvement explains only 2.4 percentage points of 31.3\% C\% (pseudo $R^2 = 0.0007$; counterfactual C\% $= 29.0\%$). Thus 92.4\% of the instability reflects item-specific changes unrelated to aggregate domain-level gains. The qualitative conclusion is robust across ETS thresholds: C\% $= 48.9\%$ at $|\Delta_{\mathrm{MH}}| \geq 1.0$, $18.3\%$ at $\geq 2.0$, and $10.2\%$ at $\geq 2.5$. \Cref{tab:temporal_landscape} summarizes the DIF landscape across all cohort comparisons.

\subsection{Ground Truth Mismatch}
\label{sec:groundtruth}

The enrichment ratio of DIF flags against the web-overlap labels of \citet{li2024contamination} is 0.96 (\Cref{tab:temporal_landscape}), indistinguishable from chance. This null result does not indicate detection failure: semi-synthetic injection produces a clear dose-response ($\Delta\mathrm{TPR} = {+}0.508$ at $p = 0.5$, ${+}0.674$ at $p = 1.0$; \Cref{fig:dose_response}). The disconnect arises because web-overlap labels measure \emph{global exposure} (whether an item exists in training corpora), while DIF measures \emph{differential functioning} (whether an item's relationship to ability changes across cohorts). An item can be globally contaminated yet function identically if all cohorts have comparable access. The Li et al.\ labels are the wrong ground truth for a differential method: the near-chance enrichment is expected, not evidence of failure.

\subsection{Structural Analysis}
\label{sec:structural}

Three progressively refined approaches (matched-ability quintiles, IRT $\theta$-conditioning, within-subject DIF) all fail to reduce the temporal FPR below 0.31. $\theta$-conditioning achieves $r(\theta, \mathrm{total\ score}) = 0.9986$ yet FPR $= 0.312$, and within-subject C\% $= 38.1\%$ exceeds the 25\% bifactor viability threshold. Yen's Q3 diagnostic reveals mild local dependence within subjects (11.3\% of pairs exceed $|Q3| > 0.20$, vs.\ 2.2\% cross-subject; $p = 2.3 \times 10^{-9}$), but cross-subject matching addresses this bias and temporal C\% persists. \Cref{app:structural} reports full results. As a further robustness check, logistic regression DIF on 9 MMLU subjects (1,787 items) confirms 92.1\% concordance between MH ETS~C and LR uniform DIF classifications and reveals additional non-uniform DIF (24.8\% of items at $|\beta_{\mathrm{interaction}}| \geq 0.5$) that MH is structurally unable to detect. The 31\% uniform DIF estimate is therefore conservative.

\subsection{Semi-Synthetic Framework Validation}
\label{sec:semisynthetic}

The critical methodological finding is that the choice of matching strategy determines validity. Cross-subject matching computes the conditioning variable from subjects \emph{other than} the one being tested, achieving $\Delta\mathrm{FPR} = 0.000$ across all five MMLU subjects while four of five produce $\Delta\mathrm{TPR} > 0.3$ at $p = 0.5$. Within-subject matching inflates FPR by ${+}0.186$ on average because the matching variable absorbs part of the injection signal. We adopt cross-subject matching as the standard protocol (\Cref{app:fig_matching}).

\subsection{Downstream Impact}
\label{sec:downstream}

Spearman correlation between full and stable-item rankings is $\rho \approx 0.997$: aggregate model ordering is preserved despite 31\% item instability. DIF-flagged items nonetheless carry more discriminative signal than average ($z = -8$ to $-49$ vs.\ random removal across 21 conditions), though a circularity ablation shows DIF-based removal is the \emph{least} disruptive among non-random strategies (Jaccard overlap with high-discrimination items is only 0.12). Individual model families show distinctive DIF profiles: Phi-3 and Llama-3 gain ${+}700$ to ${+}850$ rank positions under stable-item evaluation, while Mistral-based merges lose $-400$ to $-587$ positions (\Cref{app:fig_downstream}). The DIF instability pattern extends beyond MMLU; \Cref{sec:gsm8k} presents a full replication on GSM8K.

\subsection{Cross-Benchmark Validation: GSM8K}
\label{sec:gsm8k}

Replication on GSM8K (1,319 items $\times$ 6,702 models) yields temporal C\% $= 18.9\%$ (sanity check: 0.3\%), with semi-synthetic $\Delta\mathrm{TPR} = {+}0.514$ at $p = 0.5$ under external matching (\Cref{app:gsm8k_details}). The GSM1K held-out set~\citep{zhang2024gsm1k} provides independent contamination validation: for $N = 33$ models, the correlation between contamination gap and DIF ranking advantage is $r = -0.478$ ($p = 0.005$; partial $r = -0.385$, $p = 0.027$ after controlling for accuracy). This \emph{negative} correlation contradicts the hypothesis that DIF items benefit contaminated models, and instead suggests that DIF captures genuine capability change distinct from memorization (\Cref{fig:gsm1k}). The result is suggestive at $N = 33$ and warrants replication.
